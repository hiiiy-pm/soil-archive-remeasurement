# Harvard Forest duplicate-assay validation

This local analysis specification was fixed on 19 November 2026 before running any selection policy or scoring held-out differences. The unsigned assay-pair limits and the allocation roles are computed by `calibrate.py` without reading held-out carbon values into the analysis. This is a retrospective replay of published observations. The original seven-archive comparisons are separate.

## Source and eligibility

Use 2019 soil carbon from Harvard Forest HF449 and sample-matched loss on ignition (LOI) from HF431, with the two file hashes in `calibrate.py`. The physical unit is one site–plot–horizon–month specimen. The endpoint is mean total C in duplicate tins, in g C/kg dry soil. Divide the archive’s micrograms of C by milligrams of dry soil for each tin. The two nominal mg/g concentration columns store mass fractions and are checked after multiplying by 1,000. This unit check used calibration samples before any held-out outcome was evaluated. Use July and October, both warming treatments, both experiments (Prospect Hill and SWaN), and both horizons. Join on the sample identifier and verify site, plot, month, treatment and horizon agreement. Treat neighbouring experiments as one external forest archive. A specimen purchase buys both tin readings, with equal cost for every strategy.

## Plot split and fixed limits

In each of the four site-by-warming groups, reserve two of the five plots solely for precision calibration and auxiliary calibration. Choose the pair that leaves the greatest minimum number of complete July–October plots across the two horizons, then the greatest total number of complete plot–horizon pairs. Resolve remaining ties by the ascending numerical pair of reserved plot IDs. Use only design metadata for this split. Use all available duplicate specimens on reserved plots. On the other plots, admit only a plot–horizon present in both seasons. Both seasonal means thus comprise the same plots. List unused specimens.

Separately in each horizon, let m be the number of calibration specimens and let s = sqrt[sum(C1 − C2)²/(2m)] be the single-determination within-specimen standard deviation. For a difference between the two means of n matched plots, each assayed in duplicate, fix the two-sided analytical limit t(0.975, m) × s / sqrt(n). The reference clears the limit only when its absolute value is strictly greater. The multiplier allows for estimation of precision from m duplicate differences under independent, equal-variance, approximately normal tin errors with zero mean difference. Give each horizon its own precision. Retain all calibration pairs, including large discrepancies. Examine signed tin differences and leave-one-pair-out ranges as precision diagnostics; neither changes the cutoff. A prespecified stricter sensitivity uses t(0.975, m) × s, the analogous limit for two individual specimens each reported as a duplicate mean. A second descriptive analysis uses all nonzero differences. The limits cover analytical variation, conditional on the archived specimens. Field variation, management relevance and systematic shifts between analytical batches are outside their scope.

Fit a through-origin conversion from LOI percent to mean duplicate C using the calibration plots only, separately by horizon. The preselection auxiliary equals this coefficient times the independently recorded LOI. HF431 TOC and all HF449 carbon-derived and standardized indices are excluded from selector inputs. The choice of a subset of specimens for analysis assumes LOI is already available for the eligible frame. The calibration plots and their assays are external inputs to the replay and are outside the stated validation budgets.

## Allocation and endpoints

Use the existing unmodified joint maximin, magnitude-only and direct sign rules, both alternating orders, and uniform random sampling without replacement. Use one carbon channel, four site-by-horizon reporting domains, treatment and seasonal main effects with the published acquisition weights, and the common ordinary-kriging estimator and fixed nugget-to-signal ratio 1. Use site-specific plot and treatment identifiers, horizon, month and the calibrated LOI in the covariance. For the alternating strategy average the two orders within each pilot. Include all eligible treatment and seasonal targets during selection. The scorer alone uses the limits and the census values.

Use 200 independent pilot seeds 492701 through 492900, the same pilot for every rule in each replay. Break allocation ties with seed 100000 plus the pilot seed; complete the random path with seed 200000 plus the pilot seed. Round half up at each 10% budget step from 10% to 100%. The primary comparison is joint minus magnitude in the percentage of wrong seasonal signs among contrasts clearing the mean-level analytical limit. Count zero predictions for a nonzero truth as wrong. For each start average equally across eligible seasonal contrasts, then across nine partial budgets including the shared pilot. Report a two-sided 95% paired Monte Carlo t interval across the 200 start-level differences. The interval concerns random pilot variation in this fixed archive. Report all eight measured differences with their thresholds, comparison counts and errors at the other gates, individual budget results and the zero-error census. Report a positive difference as greater error under joint selection, including unfavourable or inconclusive results. Do not retune the rule or change the threshold after scoring.

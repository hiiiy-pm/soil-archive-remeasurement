# Soil-water isotope replay protocol

The design in design.md is applied to the archived soil-water distillates of Kelson et al. (2026). The local lock records the source checksums, calibration measurements, numerical limits, sample frame, policies and scoring code before any policy comparison on the two test regions. This retrospective test has no external preregistration timestamp.

## Source and assays

Use the CC BY 4.0 data release of Julia R. Kelson, CZ17O_soilwater, tag v2.1, Zenodo, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20532592. The accompanying study is Kelson et al. (2026), Triple Oxygen and Hydrogen Isotopes of Soil Water Across Climates, Seasons, and Depths, https://doi.org/10.1029/2026GC012921. Two files from the data release are supplied verbatim in source/. They are sw.csv and TableSX_CZ17O_TripleO_iso_stds_reps.xlsx. The data tables and outputs accompanying this replay are derived from those files. The original sample records and workbooks remain the authors' work.

Water was cryogenically distilled from actual soil samples collected at different depths. The pre-existing auxiliary is deuterium excess in per mil, calculated consistently as the laser spectrometer (CRDS) measurements of δ²H minus eight times δ¹⁸O. The deposited d_excess.mean differs from that identity in calibration sample sw50. Use the defining equation throughout. The specialized reference is Δ′¹⁷O in per meg. It is determined independently by fluorination and dual-inlet isotope-ratio mass spectrometry (IRMS). Use the unflagged D17O.SMOWSLAP.per.meg values in the workbook. Accept only analytical entries for the listed sample ID or its long alias; check the number of measurements against N.IRMS. Check that their mean reproduces D17O_pmg. The source investigators deliberately selected specimens spanning high and low conventional isotope values. Inference is conditional on this selected archive of already extracted soil waters.

## Split and repeatability

Use all 11 dual-assay soils from Jornada (New Mexico) and the Edwin S. George Reserve (Michigan) to estimate one linear regression with an intercept, converting archival CRDS deuterium excess to the reference scale. Ten of these soils have two accepted independent IRMS determinations and one has one. With so few pairs in the separate regions and depth classes, pool all ten pairs for the within-distillate analytical variance. Compute s² = Σ(a−b)²/(2m), m=10, from paired determinations on the same extracted water. This measures analytical repeatability after extraction. Repeated field cores, heterogeneity of depth bands, water extraction variability and laboratory shifts shared by both determinations are outside its scope.

The 25 test specimens come from the Mojave (California) and Reynolds Creek (Idaho). Each has two accepted determinations. One budget unit buys both IRMS determinations on a single previously extracted sample. The prior CRDS measurements and out-of-region calibration are outside this incremental assay budget. No treatment or long-term plot identity is inferred from the spatial pattern. Set the model's plot identifier to the unique specimen ID so that separate samples acquire no extra same-plot covariance.

## Strata, contrasts and cutoffs

Use two depth classes according to the released bottom of the sampled interval, shallow at or above 30 cm and deep below 30 cm. These classes are depth bands, not mapped soil horizons. Treat the three Mojave localities (Creosote, Joshua Tree and Pinyon Juniper) separately and keep Nancy Gulch at Reynolds Creek distinct. A campaign is a collection year and meteorological season (DJF, MAM, JJA or SON). December belongs to the winter ending in the following year. A comparison describes the measured specimens in the corresponding archive cells. Cells may contain a single specimen and the exact depths may differ within a band between visits.

The scoring program forms every supported within-locality temporal contrast and every co-campaign locality contrast separately within each depth class. Fully observed temporal means for n₁ and n₂ distinct specimens have analytical limit t(0.975,10) × s × √[1/(2n₁) + 1/(2n₂)]. The two in each denominator reflects averaging two IRMS runs per specimen. The calibration stage writes every numerical limit using only the cell sizes and the reserved calibration records. Restrict the primary sign endpoint to different meteorological seasons and to absolute differences strictly exceeding the frozen limits. Retain all supported temporal and co-campaign locality contrasts, including same-season comparisons across years, for acquisition and combined scaled MSE. Apply the equal-depth scoring weights, common proportional quotas, paired pilots, comparators, tie rules and multiplicity adjustment in design.md. Do not change scope or thresholds in response to observed performance.

Run prepare.py freeze before prepare.py references. The first command calculates the calibration, census of available specimens, and contrast limits. The second verifies the lock and then exports test assay values. During allocation each selector receives only current purchases and the frozen ancillary information. The subsequent scorer can use the withheld reference table for all specimens.
